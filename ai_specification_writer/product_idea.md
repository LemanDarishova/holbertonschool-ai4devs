Product Idea
Product Name
BakuFlood AI — Intelligent Urban Flood Monitoring and Early Warning System for Baku
Vision
To develop an intelligent system that monitors and predicts urban flooding risks in Baku using Internet of Things (IoT) sensors, Artificial Intelligence (AI), and Geographic Information Systems (GIS). The product aims to improve public safety, reduce traffic disruptions, and minimize infrastructure damage by providing timely, location-specific warnings to residents and responsible authorities.
Problem and Context
During heavy rainfall, water accumulates on certain streets in Baku, making transportation difficult, creating safety hazards, and potentially damaging urban infrastructure. The 2026–2035 State Program concerning water supply, stormwater, and wastewater infrastructure in Baku and the Absheron Peninsula highlights the importance of improving water management systems.

BakuFlood AI is designed to complement existing drainage infrastructure through continuous monitoring, predictive risk assessment, and faster communication. It does not replace physical drainage improvements.
Users
- Residents and pedestrians: Need timely warnings about flooded or high-risk streets to plan safer journeys.
- Drivers and transportation service providers: Need reliable information about road conditions to avoid flooded routes, delays, and possible vehicle damage.
- Municipal and urban infrastructure authorities: Need real-time visibility into flood-prone locations to prioritize drainage maintenance and emergency response.
- Emergency response services: Need early alerts and location-specific risk information to coordinate interventions more effectively.
- System administrators and data analysts: Need to monitor sensor health, maintain data quality, and evaluate risk predictions.
Key Features
1. Real-Time IoT Water-Level Monitoring: Collect water-level measurements from sensors installed at flood-prone streets, drainage channels, and other critical locations.
2. Weather Data Integration: Retrieve rainfall measurements, forecasts, and relevant meteorological information from trusted data providers.
3. AI-Based Flood Risk Prediction: Analyze water levels, weather conditions, historical observations, and geographical characteristics to estimate flood risk in specific areas. A rule-based method can be used initially if sufficient training data is unavailable.
4. Interactive GIS Risk Map: Display monitored streets and locations with clear risk categories (low, medium, and high), including the latest data update time.
5. Early Warning Notifications: Send location-specific alerts to authorized public agencies and subscribed users through supported channels such as mobile push notifications or SMS when risk thresholds are exceeded.
6. Administrative Dashboard: Provide responsible organizations with a centralized view of current risks, active alerts, sensor status, and incident history.
7. Historical Data and Analytics: Identify recurring flood hotspots, generate reports, and support data-driven infrastructure planning.
8. Sensor Health and Data Reliability Monitoring: Detect missing, delayed, or abnormal sensor readings and indicate when risk estimates are based on incomplete data.
Differentiators
- Designed for Baku: Tailored to local flood-prone streets, drainage conditions, and rainfall patterns.
- Multiple Data Sources: Combines physical water-level sensor readings, meteorological information, and geospatial data rather than relying on weather forecasts alone.
- Predictive, Not Only Reactive: Aims to warn users about emerging risks before flooding becomes severe.
- Public and Institutional Value: Offers understandable alerts for residents and operational insights for public authorities.
- Scalable Implementation: Can begin as a pilot in selected high-risk streets and expand across the city after field testing and validation.
Expected Impact
- Earlier and more targeted awareness of potential flooding.
- Safer route decisions for pedestrians and drivers.
- Faster, better-informed response by responsible authorities.
- A historical evidence base for prioritizing drainage and infrastructure improvements.
Scope and Assumptions
This document defines the initial product concept rather than a final engineering design. Sensor locations and quantities, risk thresholds, model accuracy targets, access to meteorological data, and supported notification channels will be specified and validated in later requirements and technical specification stages.
